Situation map · Developing story

A wall of water on the Bhotekoshi

On the morning of 26 August, an earthquake shook the glaciers above Gyirong County, Tibet. Seven minutes later a flash flood — reportedly rising ~80 m above river level — tore through the Nepal–China border at Rasuwagadhi and ran the length of the Trishuli river. This page maps where it began, how it unfolded, and where the water went.

How it happened

1

An earthquake strikes the high Himalaya

At 08:37 NPT a quake hits beneath Gyirong County, Tibet — the glaciated headwaters of the Lhende Khola, about 65 km north of Kathmandu.

2

An ice-rock avalanche lets go

A mass of ice and rock — reported ~600 m wide — falls roughly 1,200 m from the glacier into the narrow river gorge below.

3

The river is dammed, then bursts

ICIMOD experts say the debris briefly blocked the Lhende Khola. Water pooled behind it, then broke through as a single surge.

4

The surge runs 100+ km downstream

Seven minutes after the quake the wave hit Rasuwagadhi, then swept Timure and Syaphrubesi, wrecked six power facilities along the Trishuli, and pushed high water all the way to the Narayani in Chitwan.
